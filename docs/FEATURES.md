## Extract: Read a drawing or a structured file into a draft asset model

<a id="drawing-to-bundle"></a>
### A P&ID image to a draft asset model and review bundle

Example review: **reviewed example**

**Why it matters:** Retyping drawings into asset lists takes manual effort in historian, HMI and digital twin projects. A draft turns some of that typing into checking; the time saved has not been measured.

**Inputs:** One P&ID as a PNG; a model subscription (or the committed example)

**Outputs:** Extracted symbols, tags and links as a draft model, a review sheet, Ignition tags and PI staging. Your own-drawing sheet has no research-trained risk tiers; review priorities need your engineering judgment

**Time:** 88 s of commands on one new sheet (sheet 8), measured once; sheet 0's recorded run took 75 s plus 1 s; review time not measured

- **Tools:** `run_extraction.py`, `trace_connections.py`, `scan_cleanup.py`, `import_site_drawing.py`, `site_extraction_workflow.py`

<a id="drawing-set-to-bundle"></a>
### Several P&ID sheets to one bundle with cross-sheet pairs and a tag check

Example review: not yet: needs review

**Why it matters:** Plants are drawn across many sheets, and a line or loop that leaves one sheet continues on another. A tag printed in the PDF is a free, exact check on what the reader extracted.

**Inputs:** Each sheet as a PNG with its extraction, drawing number and title; the original PDF for the tag check

**Outputs:** One draft model across the sheets with off-page connectors paired by drawing number, title or system code (ties left for a person), the drawn pipe segments and instrument connections as graph relations, a report with every sheet and the pairs, and per-sheet lists of tags the reader missed or may have misread. Pages without a text layer are reported as needing OCR

**Time:** Three public Pahala WWTP sheets read in 183 s plus 9 s of tracing, measured once; intake, report and tag check took seconds; review time not measured

- **Tools:** `import_drawing_set.py`, `offpage_pairing.py`, `line_layers.py`, `pdf_tag_check.py`, `deliverable_report.py`

<a id="dexpi-xml-to-model"></a>
### Your own DEXPI XML export to a process model with its attributes kept

Example review: **reviewed example**

**Why it matters:** A CAD export can hold sizes, specifications and design limits. The reader preserves supported attributes with XML element citations and reports folds, placement gaps and omissions. Explicit source capture also retains the complete policy-filtered structured packet, distinguishing Native mappings from retained but uninterpreted occurrences.

**Inputs:** A DEXPI P&ID XML file (tested on DEXPI 1.3, and on DEXPI 1.2 exports from SPPID, Comos, AVEVA and AutoCAD); a rendered PNG only if you also want the drawing bundle

**Outputs:** A Native process model and conversion report. Existing downstream tools can use eligible Native fields after their own validation; some converted models lack the equipment or instruments a tool requires. Optional explicit capture appends a complete policy-filtered source packet; graph/context/MCP consumers do not expose that packet.

**Time:** Seconds per sheet

- **Tools:** `dexpi_reader.py`

<a id="document-text"></a>
### Read the text inside PDF, Word, Excel, PowerPoint and HTML documents, with a locator for every piece

Example review: not yet: needs review

**Why it matters:** Procedures, PHAs, CAPAs and work orders name the same equipment as the P&ID and the instrument index, but they live in Office files and PDFs that a text search cannot see. A locator on every piece lets a reviewer open the document at the place a tag was found.

**Inputs:** The documents (.pdf, .docx/.docm, .xlsx/.xlsm, .pptx/.pptm, .html, .txt, .md, .csv); for PDF text, the optional pypdf package or a pdftotext program. Optional, only when asked for: Tesseract (and Poppler pdftoppm) for OCR of scanned pages and images, LibreOffice for legacy .doc/.xls/.ppt/.rtf

**Outputs:** One text file per document with a locator before each piece (page 3, paragraph 12, table 2 row 4, sheet 'PM plan' cell B7, slide 4, line 9) and a manifest with each file's SHA-256, status and warnings. Embedded Office/PDF files are read with their place (embedded 'spec.pdf' (in table 3) page 2). Scanned pages are reported as needing OCR and legacy .doc/.xls with a conversion step, unless the optional --ocr auto or --convert-legacy add-ons and their programs are used; --pdf-layout and --excel-display are further opt-ins. Prototype on fictional and public files; OCR and conversion tested with stand-in programs and, where installed, the real Tesseract, Poppler and LibreOffice (OCR accuracy measured on rendered and scanned public pages); no plant acceptance

**Time:** Seconds per document

- **Tools:** `extract_text.py`
- **How to use it:** [DOCUMENT_TEXT.md](../docs/DOCUMENT_TEXT.md)

### First-draft annual energy, cost and CO2e from a measured change, with its assumptions and the data to firm it up

Example review: **reviewed example**

**Why it matters:** A measured change becomes a first-draft cost and emissions figure, good enough to decide whether to collect better data, with every constant's source shown and every assumption listed. If you don't have a constant, it tells you exactly which bill, contract or public table has it.

**Inputs:** What you measured (kW before and after, steam lb/h, fuel saved, hours) and your tariff, gas price, emission factors and boiler efficiency, or labelled public typical values

**Outputs:** A first-draft estimate, marked as one: annual kWh or MMBtu, cost and CO2e with the formula and source of every constant, the assumptions it rests on, and the data to collect (and where) to replace each one; or, per question, what to measure and where each constant comes from

**Time:** Minutes

- **Tools:** `estimate_impact.py`
- **How to use it:** [IMPACT_ESTIMATE.md](../docs/IMPACT_ESTIMATE.md)
